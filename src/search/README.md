# src/search — Stage 8. The ROUTER over the index stage 1 already built: `card_fts` is FTS5 with `tokenize='trigram'` and so cannot serve a query under three characters, while two characters is the commonest search unit in Japanese — so a 1–2 character query goes to a ranked LIKE scan over the `card_search_index` view and 3+ goes to the trigram index, and `SearchResult.mode` says which ran. Both paths return the same columns, both rank (bm25 for FTS, bm25's own three ingredients computed by hand for the scan), both report an exact `total` separately from the page, and neither orders by `card_id`. Search sees `cards.search_text` — the rendered, zero-width-stripped form — which is why `～に[[引き|10PQotxm]]‎[[換え|C1p9g1mL]]` is findable as 引き換え and why the opaque link ids are findable not at all: of the 2,895 distinct `card_links.to_card_id` values, a trigram index over the raw concatenation would match 2,870 across 7,801 card hits, and `card_fts` matches 0. Every metacharacter the corpus actually contains (`%`, `_`, `"`, `*`, `AND`, `col:`, and the `\*` jisho wildcards in his own cards) is escaped to a literal rather than to an operator — `escapeLike` and `ftsPhrase` in `query.ts`. `index.ts` re-exports; `query.ts` routes and escapes; `sql.ts` builds both statements and holds every ordering constant in one place; `snippet.ts` excerpts around the match and reports where it landed; `search.ts` executes. It answers queries and nothing more: no palette, no debounce, no history, no create-on-no-match (stage 10 uses `total` and `truncated` from here to know that "no other matches" is true).
